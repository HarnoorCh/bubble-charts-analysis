USER: shoudln't p50 be = average?
ASSISTANT: No — they're only equal for a **symmetric** distribution. Mean and median measure different things: - **Mean (average)** = sum ÷ count; every value pulls on it, so a few large outliers drag it up. - **p50 (median)** = the middle value; half the orders are below, half above. Outliers don't move it. "Time from a sub-5 request to delivery" is **right-skewed** — most orders land in a few minutes, but a long tail of stragglers (riders stuck, reassignments, 20–40 min waits) stretches the upper end. That tail inflates the mean but barely touches the median, so here you'd expect **p50 < mean**. Rough illustration — five orders, minutes-to-delivery `[3, 4, 5, 6, 27]`: - mean = 45 ÷ 5 = **9.0** (the single 27 drags it up) - median = middle value = **5.0** That gap is exactly why I suggested adding p50: it tells you the *typical* customer experience, while the mean tells you the average including the painful tail. If the two come out close, the distribution is fairly symmetric; if p50 is well below the mean, you've got a skewed tail worth looking at separately. For these ETA-vs-reality gaps I'd expect a visible spread, so they won't match.
